What tools.make can make

22 kinds. A spec of a few lines names one; python -m tools.make spec.yaml writes the file beside it, checked as everything the kit makes is. --schema KIND prints a kind's fields and its example.

erd

A database diagram: tables, their columns and keys, the references between them.

Fields of erd
A database diagram: each table a box of its columns -- keys marked, types to the right, a nullable
one with a ? -- laid out by its foreign keys, a table to the left of the ones it refers to. Each
reference runs from its column to the column it names, a crow's foot at the many end and a bar at
the one.

  tables:   name: {column: "type [pk] [fk table.column] [null]", …}
            or a column as {type, pk, fk, null}; columns keep their order
  source:   a .sql file instead (or as well): its CREATE TABLE statements -- columns, PRIMARY KEY,
            REFERENCES, FOREIGN KEY … REFERENCES, and ALTER TABLE … ADD FOREIGN KEY
  only:     [table names] -- draw these alone (from a large source)
  types:    false leaves the types out

flow

Boxes and arrows (an architecture, a request flow, a pipeline), laid out by itself.

Fields of flow
A diagram of boxes and arrows -- an architecture, a request flow, a pipeline -- laid out by itself:
boxes in columns along the flow, as few crossings as it finds, arrows routed square between them.

  nodes:      id: "Label"  -- or id: {label, sub, kind, icon, shape, state, count, tag, at}
              kind, icon and shape are guessed from the words when not given ("Postgres" is a
              database cylinder, "Redis" a cache, "Orders queue" a queue, "Browser" a client).
              kind: client | edge | compute | cache | data | async | external
              state: normal | dim | focus | fail | warn | good;  icon: false for none
              at: [column, row] pins a box (0-based); boxes without one are placed around it
  edges:      "a -> b: label"   --> dashed (a path not always taken), <-> both ways, -- no head;
              a label in `backticks` is set as code. Or {from, to, label, dash, both, head, color,
              step}; color: good | bad | warn | info | accent | muted | ink
  groups:     [{title, nodes: [ids], icon, color}] -- a dashed region round its boxes
  direction:  right (the default) | down
  numbered:   true numbers the edges in the order written (a step marker on each)
  box:        {w, h} -- every box's size; by default the widest label decides
  gap:        {along, across} -- the space between columns (at least) and between rows

The figure is checked as it is drawn. A label that collides is usually a gap too small for it:
give the gap more room, or shorten the label.

quadrant

A 2x2: items placed by two measures, the four quadrants named.

Fields of quadrant
A 2x2: items placed by two measures on a square split into four, each quadrant named in its corner.
Each item's label goes in the first place round its dot where it meets no other label, dot, name or
line.

  x:          the measure across: "Effort" -- or {label, low, high}: the words at its two ends
  y:          the measure up: the same
  quadrants:  four names: [top left, top right, bottom left, bottom right]
  items:      [{label, x, y, tone}] -- or [label, x, y]; tone good | bad | warn | info colours its dot
  range:      {x: [low, high], y: [low, high]} -- the scale x and y are given in (0 to 1 by default)
  size:       the square's side in px (420)

sequence

Who sends what to whom, in order: a request flow, a handshake, a protocol.

Fields of sequence
A sequence diagram: who sends what to whom, in order, down the page. Lifelines are spaced by the
labels between them; a message that passes a lifeline puts its label in a gap; the canvas fits.

  actors:    id: "Label" -- or id: {label, kind, icon}; kind and icon guessed from the words
  messages:  in order, each one of
             "a -> b: label"        a call (numbered)
             "b --> a: label"       a return (dashed)
             "a -> a: label"        a call to itself
             "== text"              a divider across every lifeline: a phase, a pause
             and a message may end with "| a note" (set small, under it) and "[good]", "[bad]" or
             "[warn]" (its colour); a label in `backticks` is set as code.
             Or {from, to, label, ret, note, tone, mono}.
  spans:     [[actor, first message, last message], …] -- an activation bar (messages count from 1)
  brackets:  [{actor, from, to, text, side}] -- a bracket beside a lifeline over messages from..to,
             side left (the default) or right: a time, a round trip
  gap:       the space between two messages (36)

state

A state machine: states, the events between them, where it starts and ends.

Fields of state
A state machine: the states something passes through and the events that move it, laid out by
itself along the way forward; a transition that goes back runs round below, one to the same state
is a loop on its top.

  states:       id: "Label" -- or id: {label, sub, kind, state, icon}
                kind sets the colour (compute by default); state: normal | dim | focus | fail |
                warn | good, as a box's; no icon unless one is given
  transitions:  "a -> b: event" -- --> dashed (a path not always taken); or {from, to, label,
                dash, color}; a label in `backticks` is set as code
  initial:      the state it starts in: a filled dot with an arrow into it
  final:        [states] it can end in: drawn with a double border
  direction:    right (the default) | down
  box:          {w, h} -- every state's size; by default the widest label decides

timeline

Milestones and phases on a dated axis: a roadmap, a plan, a history.

Fields of timeline
A timeline: milestones as dots on a dated axis, phases as bars in lanes under it, and today marked.
Milestone labels that would collide are lifted onto rows of their own; a phase's label sits in its
bar, or beside it when the bar is short.

  milestones:  [{date, label, tone}] -- or "2026-11-20: Beta"; tone good | bad | warn | info
  phases:      [{start, end, label, lane, kind}] -- a bar from start to end; phases of one lane
               share its row (moved to a second row where they overlap); kind colours the bar
               (client | edge | compute | cache | data | async | external), by lane by default
  today:       a date: a dashed line across the axis and the lanes
  width:       the figure's width in px (760)

Dates are written YYYY-MM-DD. The axis is marked by week or by month, whichever the span reads in.

tree

A tree from an indented outline: a folder tree, an org chart, a decision tree.

Fields of tree
A tree from an indented outline: a folder tree, an org chart or a decision tree. Each line is one
item; an item indented under another is its child (any indent, used consistently).

  style:      folder (the default) | org | decision
  text:       the outline, as a block (text: |). In each line:
                "name  # comment"   a comment, set muted to the right (folder)
                "name/"             a folder (folder; set bold)
                "Label | sub"       a second, smaller line in the box (org, decision)
                "[yes] Label"       the label on the line into the item (decision, org)
                "Label !good"       a box's state: good | warn | fail | focus | dim (org, decision)
  direction:  down (the default) | right -- which way an org or decision tree grows
  kind:       the boxes' kind (org and decision questions): client | edge | compute | cache |
              data | async | external -- compute by default
  leaf:       the kind of a decision tree's answers (its items with no children): data by default
  box:        {w, h} -- every box's size; by default the widest label decides

chart

A chart -- line, bar, stacked, area, scatter, waterfall, heatmap -- from numbers or a file.

Fields of chart
A chart from numbers written in the spec or read from a CSV or Excel file, in the theme's style;
lines and bars labelled directly rather than through a legend where they can be.

  type:     line | bar | hbar | stacked | area | scatter | waterfall | heatmap
  title:    what to see (not what the axes are)
  x:        the categories or x values: a list -- or, with data:, a column's name
  series:   name: [values] in order, one per line or bar group -- or, with data:, a list of columns
  data:     a .csv, .xlsx or .xls file beside the spec (sheet: for a sheet of a workbook)
  xlabel, ylabel:  the axes' titles;  unit: put after each value written on a bar (" ms", " %")
  ylim:     [low, high];  size: [width, height] in inches (6.4 x 2.6 fills an A4 column)
  marks:    [{y, label}] a threshold across the chart, or [{x, label}] a moment along it
  values:   true writes each bar's value on it
  legend:   true puts a legend in place of labels at the lines' ends (when they would crowd)
  colors:   [series colour names], in the order of series: blue teal green orange violet slate
            (the theme's colours by technical's hue names), or good | bad | warn | muted

  waterfall:  steps: {Start: 100, Sales: +20, Costs: -15, End: total} -- "total" closes the run
  heatmap:    rows: [names], cols: [names], values: [[…] per row]; scale: sequential | diverging;
              kind: the kind whose colour a sequential scale ends in (compute, async, data, …)
  scatter:    series: {name: [[x, y], …]};  labels: [text per point of the first series]

adr

An architecture decision record: context, drivers, options, decision, consequences.

adr.html
The page it makes: open it to see it at full size.
Fields of adr
An architecture decision record: what was decided, in what context, between which options, and what
follows from it -- the Nygard form with MADR's drivers and options. Numbered, dated and with its
status shown, so a reader knows at once whether it still holds.

  number:      the record's number (12 -> ADR-012)
  title:       the decision, short ("Use SQS FIFO for order events")
  status:      proposed | accepted | superseded | deprecated | rejected
  date:        when it took that status;  deciders: [names or roles]
  supersedes:  "ADR-007" (or a list);  superseded_by: "ADR-019" -- shown beside the status
  context:     Markdown: the forces at play, the problem
  drivers:     [what the decision must achieve or respect]
  options:     [{name, summary, pros: [...], cons: [...]}] -- the ones considered
  decision:    {option, why} -- the option chosen (one of the options' names) and why;
               or Markdown, when no options are listed
  consequences: {good: [...], bad: [...], neutral: [...]}
  links:       [{text, url}] or ["text: url"] -- related records, tickets, documents
  eyebrow, lead, footer, format (pdf by default | html | [html, pdf]): as every document

cheatsheet

A dense reference: sections of terms or commands, in columns on paper, in cards on a page.

cheatsheet.html
The page it makes: open it to see it at full size.
Fields of cheatsheet
A cheat sheet: a dense reference to keep beside the keyboard -- sections of terms or commands, each with
what it does, and a snippet where one says it better. As a PDF it is set small in two or three
columns; as a page, its sections are cards in a grid that reflows to the width of the screen.

  title:     what it covers ("Git, the commands of a working week")
  sections:  [{title, items, md, code, lang}]
             items: [[term, what it does], …] -- or [{term, text, code, lang}]; `text` is Markdown
             inline, `code` a snippet shown under it
             md: Markdown after the items;  code + lang: a snippet closing the section
  terms:     code (the default: terms set as code -- commands, functions) | text (words, set bold)
  columns:   2 (the default) or 3: the PDF's columns
  eyebrow, lead, footer, paper: as every document;  format: pdf (the default) | html | [pdf, html]

decision

Options scored against weighted criteria, a recommendation, and what would change it.

decision.html
The page it makes: open it to see it at full size.
Fields of decision
A decision laid out for the person who makes it: the question, what it rests on, the options scored
against weighted criteria, each option's case for and against, a recommendation -- and what would
change it. The scorecard is worked out from the scores; a recommendation that is not the top score is
said so, not hidden.

  title:           the decision, as a heading ("Which queue carries order events")
  question:        the question the reader answers (the lead, unless `lead:` is given)
  eyebrow, lead, footer, format (html | pdf | [html, pdf]), contents, dense: as every document
  context:         Markdown: why this is being decided now, what it rests on
  criteria:        name: weight, in order of importance -- or [{name, weight, note}]; weights are
                   relative (3 counts three times 1)
  options:         [{name, summary, scores: {criterion: 1-5}, pros: [...], cons: [...], effort, cost}]
                   every option scores every criterion, 1 (poor) to 5 (strong)
  recommendation:  {option, why}: the option recommended (one of the options' names) and why
  change_mind:     [what would change the recommendation]
  risks:           [risks of the recommended option]
  next:            [next steps, in order, once decided]
  decide_by:       when the decision is needed;  owner: who makes it

doc

A page or PDF from blocks and Markdown: a report, a design note, a handout, a one-pager.

doc.html
The page it makes: open it to see it at full size.
Fields of doc
A document -- a report, a design note, a handout, a one-pager -- as an HTML page, a PDF or both, from
blocks written in the spec, a Markdown file, or both. Figures are other specs (a file, or inline),
drawn as the document is made.

  title:     the title (from a Markdown source's first `# heading` when not given)
  eyebrow:   the small line above it;  lead: a paragraph under it (Markdown inline)
  footer:    the line at the foot of a page or every PDF page
  format:    html (the default) | pdf | [html, pdf]
  template:  page (the default) | report (a contents bar on the page) | one-pager (dense: a PDF
             that must hold a lot on one page)
  contents:  true gives the page a contents bar of its h2s;  dense: true tightens a PDF
  source:    a Markdown file whose text comes first (before any blocks)
  blocks:    the body, one block each -- see the blocks below

Blocks (each a mapping whose first key says what it is):

  h2: text | h3: text              a heading
  p: text                          a paragraph; Markdown inline (**bold**, `code`, [links](…))
  md: text | md: notes.md          Markdown: paragraphs, lists, tables, code, images
  kpis: [[label, value, note, tone], …]     stat tiles; tone good | warn | bad | info | neutral
  callout: text, title:, tone:     an aside; tone info | good | warn | bad
  table: {header, rows, numeric: [column numbers]} -- or table: data.csv, columns:, decimals:
  figure: chart.yaml | {make: …} | drawing.svg, caption:, width: 80% (PDF)
  row: [{figure: …, caption: …}, …]          figures side by side
  columns: [[blocks], [blocks]]    blocks side by side
  code: text | code: file.py, lang:, caption:
  image: shot.webp, caption:, alt:
  qr: https://…, caption:
  pagebreak: true                  (PDF) the next block starts a page
  page only: steps: [{figure, title, text}] · chat: [[you|them, text]], you:, them: ·
             annotated: shot.webp, marks: [[x, y, text]] · diff: {before, after}, labels: ·
             cards: [[href, title, text]], kicker: [...]

A slide deck as one HTML file: 16:9 slides in the look of the pages, scaled to the window, turned

              steps, diff, image, annotated, cards
    left, right:  [blocks] -- a two-column slide's columns (points: and figure: go on the left)

status

A weekly or monthly status: tiles worked out from targets, risks, milestones, asks.

status.html
The page it makes: open it to see it at full size.
Fields of status
A status report for a week or a month: where things stand at a glance, what moved, what is at risk,
how the milestones go, and what is needed from the reader. Figures given with what they were before
and what they aim at have their change and their colour worked out.

  title:       what is reported on ("Orders platform")
  period:      the week or month ("Week 43 · 20-24 October 2026"); the eyebrow, unless one is given
  overall:     done | on-track | at-risk | late -- the first tile
  summary:     Markdown: the state in a few sentences (one paragraph is set as the lead)
  compare:     what `previous` values are ("last week" by default)

               note ("−16 ms on last week · target 200 ms") and tone (good when the target is met,
               or when it moved the better way) are worked out unless given; or [label, value, note, tone]
  highlights:  [what went well or moved]
  risks:       [{risk, severity: high | medium | low, owner, mitigation}] -- highest first
  milestones:  [{name, due, state: done | on-track | at-risk | late, note}] -- drawn as a list with
               each state in its colour
  asks:        [decisions or help needed from the reader]
  figures:     [{figure: chart.yaml | {make: chart, …}, caption}] -- after the milestones
  eyebrow, lead, footer, format (html by default | pdf | [html, pdf]), contents, dense: as every document

explainer

A page that steps through a process over one diagram, the same boxes lit step by step.

explainer.html
The page it makes: open it to see it at full size.
Fields of explainer
A page that teaches a process over one diagram: the reader steps through frames, and in each the same
boxes stand in the same places while what the step is about lights up -- a box in focus, one that
fails, an arrow that carries the step -- so the eye sees what changed.

  title:, eyebrow:, lead:, footer:, contents:   as a document's
  diagram:   the diagram: a flow spec's file (make: flow), or its fields inline (nodes, edges,
             groups, direction, numbered, box, gap -- see `--schema flow`)
  intro:     Markdown before the steps
  frames:    the steps, in order, each
               title:   the step's name
               text:    what happens in it (Markdown inline)
               focus:, fail:, warn:, good:, dim:   [box ids] in that state for this step
               rest:    dim -- every box the step does not name is dimmed (normal by default)
               highlight:   [edges] drawn in the accent colour -- or in `tone:` good | bad | warn | info
               dim_edges:   [edges] drawn faint;  hide_edges: [edges] not drawn
             an edge is named by its number in `edges` (from 1) or as "a -> b"
  caption:   a line under the steps
  after:     blocks after the steps (the doc kind's blocks: p, md, callout, table, figure, …)

Every frame is drawn from the same layout, so the boxes never move; the page is HTML only.

flashcards

Flashcards to print double-sided and cut: fronts, then their backs mirrored to line up.

Fields of flashcards
Flashcards to print and cut: a grid of cards on each page, the fronts on one page and their backs
on the next, each row mirrored left to right, so that printed on both sides (flipped on the long
edge) every back lands behind its front. Text too long for a card is set smaller until it fits.

  title:      a small line at the top of every card (the deck's name)
  cards:      [{front, back}] -- Markdown; or
  questions:  questions in the Trainings model (as the quiz kind takes them); or
  from:       a Trainings question file (questions/chNN.js), read with Node: the front is the
              question with its material (and its options, items or pairs), the back the right
              answer and the takeaway;  lessons:, levels:, ids: keep only these
  grid:       [columns, rows] on a page (2 x 4 by default)
  paper:      a4 (the default) | us-letter;  margin: in mm (10)
  backs:      false prints the fronts alone, one side only (true by default)

quiz

A quiz to print (or a page): questions in the eleven Trainings formats, and an answer key.

Fields of quiz
A quiz on paper -- or on a page -- from questions in the Trainings model (ai-workbench-learning,
Trainings/QUESTION-STANDARD.md, section 2): every one of the eleven formats set so it can be answered
with a pen, then an answer key on a new page, with why each answer is right and what to remember.

  title:      the quiz's title;  eyebrow:, lead:, footer: as a document's
  format:     pdf (the default) | html (answers folded under each question) | [pdf, html]
  key:        false leaves the answer key out (true by default)
  dense:      true tightens the PDF;  paper: a4 (the default) | us-letter | a5
  questions:  the questions, written here, each in the Trainings model:
              id, lesson, level (beginner | intermediate | advanced | expert), type,
              format, material, q (Markdown), takeaway, why -- and the format's own fields:
                choice-one, choice-many   options: [{t, ok: true, why}]
                short-answer              accept: [...], compare, traps: [{text, why}]
                numeric                   answer, unit, tolerance: {kind: absolute | relative, value}
                spot                      faulty: [part numbers], notes: {number: why}
                fill-gaps                 gaps: [{choices, answer, why}] -- {{1}}, {{2}} in the material
                order                     items (in the right order; shown shuffled), orderBy
                classify                  groups: [{name, items}]
                match                     pairs: [[left, right]]
                free-response             model (Markdown), points: [...]
                case                      scenario, steps: [questions of the other formats]
              material: {kind: none | code (lang, text) | text (text) | list (items) |
                         dialogue (turns: [{who, says}]) | table (columns, rows)}
  from:       a Trainings question file (questions/chNN.js) instead -- read with Node
  lessons:, levels:, ids:   keep only these questions (of either)

The order, classify and match formats are shuffled on paper, the same way each time (by the
question's id), and the key says the right order by the letters shown.

Fields of git-report
A page on a repository's history, read from git: how much changed, week by week, where, and the latest

impact

What a change to a product-specs ID turns stale, wave by wave, from the specification itself.

Fields of impact
What a change to one ID of a product specification reaches, in waves, read from the specification as the
product-specs skill wrote it: what the ID stands on (spec.py --impact), then the pages that turn stale when it
changes -- each page reviewed against it, in reviewed.json -- then the pages reviewed against those, which
turn stale in their turn if correcting the first wave changes what they say, and so on.

  specs:     the product-specs folder (the one holding model/, site/ and reviewed.json)
  id:        the ID that changes: R4, B1, N1, SC3 (an element or a criterion, SC3.13, counts as its page)
  depth:     how many waves to follow, 1 to 4 (2)
  stands_on: false leaves out the column of what the ID stands on (true)
  labels:    false leaves the arrows of the first wave unlabelled; later waves never carry labels (true)
  limit:     at most this many boxes in a column (10); past it, the first by ID are kept and one box
             says how many more there are

Each box is "ID · name", coloured by its kind of page (its kind written under it); the changed ID is in

licences

The licences of everything a checkout uses, from its third_party.py: tiles, tables, exceptions.

licences.html
The page it makes: open it to see it at full size.
Fields of licences
A page of everything of someone else's a checkout uses, under what licence, and whether it may be used: what
the checkout's own third_party.py finds, read by running its build() -- the same reading its --check makes,
so the page and the check never disagree. Its sections become the page's, its verdicts the tiles and a
count by source, its exceptions callouts with their reasons.

  script:   the licence script, relative to the spec (third_party.py at the top of the checkout by default)
  only:     all (the default) lists every row; problems lists only the rows not allowed outright -- a short
            page when the long tables are known to be fine
  title, eyebrow, lead, footer, format (html | pdf | [html, pdf]): as for the doc kind ("Third-party
            licences" by default)

It reads what is installed in the venv that runs tools.make and what git tracks, so the page moves when a
package is added or updated: make it again then (and --check calls it stale until then).

spec-graph

The IDs around one ID of a product specification, and how each is tied to it.

Fields of spec-graph
The neighbourhood of one ID in a product specification: the IDs its edges reach, and the edges between them,
each labelled by its relation, as the model the product-specs skill wrote holds them (model/edges.tsv).
Laid out by the flow kind: an edge runs from what names to what is named.

  specs:      the product-specs folder (the one holding model/)
  around:     the ID in the middle: SC3, R4, N1
  depth:      1 (the default): what it names and what names it; 2: and what those name and are named by
  relations:  only these relations: [shows, leads to, about] (all but `has` by default)
  leave:      relations to leave out ([has]; `has` ties a page to its own elements and parts)
  elements:   true keeps an element or a part (SC3.13) as its own box; by default it counts as its page
  limit:      at most this many boxes besides the one in the middle (14), shared between the two sides
              (half each; what one side does not use goes to the other), the first ring before the second;
              on each side those tied by the most relations are kept, then by ID, and one box says how
              many more there are -- narrow with relations: or leave: to see the rest
  direction:  right (the default) | down

Drawn as a star: what names the ID on its left, what it names on its right (an ID tied both ways on the
right, its arrow with two heads, "names / named by"); at depth 2 the next ring further out, each box tied by
the one arrow it hangs from -- ties between neighbours are left out, or the figure would be a tangle. Each
box is "ID · name", coloured by its kind of page (its kind written under it); the ID in the middle is in
focus. Two IDs tied by more than one relation share one arrow with every relation on it.
